Muhammad Ali

The Greatest · The Louisville Lip 1942 – 2016

Olympic gold · 3× World Heavyweight Champion · Activist & Icon

Who He Was

Cassius Marcellus Clay Jr. (later Muhammad Ali) was not only one of the greatest boxers in history, but also a cultural and political force. His speed, charisma, and unorthodox style revolutionised the ring. Outside it, his refusal to be drafted during the Vietnam War and his conversion to Islam made him a symbol of resistance and conviction.

Ali’s influence transcends boxing. He was a poet, a philanthropist, and a global ambassador for peace. His Parkinson’s diagnosis in 1984 did not silence him; he remained a beloved public figure until his death in 2016.

Key Fights & Milestones

  • 1960 Olympic Gold – Light heavyweight champion in Rome.
  • 1964 First Heavyweight Title – Defeats Sonny Liston (age 22).
  • 1967 Stripped of Title – Refuses induction into U.S. Army.
  • 1971 Fight of the Century – Loses to Joe Frazier (first loss).
  • 1974 Rumble in the Jungle – Knocks out George Foreman in Zaire.
  • 1975 Thrilla in Manila – Defeats Joe Frazier in brutal bout.
  • 1981 Retirement – Finishes career with 56 wins (37 KOs), 5 losses.

Legacy

Ali’s legacy is etched not only in the ring but in the hearts of millions who saw him stand for what he believed in. He used his platform to speak against racism, war, and injustice. In his own words: “Service to others is the rent you pay for your room here on earth.”

Today, he is remembered as “The People’s Champion” — a man who combined athletic brilliance with an unshakeable moral compass.
